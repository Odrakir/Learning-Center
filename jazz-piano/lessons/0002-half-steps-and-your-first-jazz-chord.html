Half Steps and Your First Jazz Chord

One interval pattern — 4, then 3, then 4 half steps — builds a major 7 chord on any root, anywhere on the keyboard.
Why this, now? You can already find any natural note by pattern (Lesson 1). Today you learn to measure the distance between notes, and use that measurement to build a chord — a major 7th (maj7), one of the three core color-chords of jazz harmony. This is the first of the three building blocks toward playing real jazz voicings and, eventually, reading a lead sheet full of chord symbols. (See your mission.)

The idea, in 90 seconds

The smallest distance between two notes on a piano is a half step — one key to the very next key, black or white, no gap. Two half steps make a whole step. That's the entire ruler jazz harmony is measured with. (Full definitions in the glossary.)

"Every major 7 chord is the same shape: root, up 4 half steps, up 3 more, up 4 more. Move the shape, the chord moves with it."

That's the trick — exactly like Lesson 1's black-key pattern. You're not memorizing four separate notes for every possible chord; you're memorizing one spacing pattern and re-planting it wherever the root lands. Starting on C: up a major 3rd (4 half steps) to E, up a minor 3rd (3 half steps) to G, up a major 3rd (4 half steps) to B. C – E – G – B is a Cmaj7 chord.

Remember Lesson 1 saying black keys were "covered in a later lesson"? This is that lesson — move the same 4-3-4 shape to a root like D or G and some of those four notes land on black keys. The shape never changes; only the keyboard picture does.

Now you try

The root is marked for you each round. Click the three notes that complete the maj7 shape (4-3-4 half steps up), then check your answer. No note names required — count the pattern.

Check yourself

Aim to get all four practice chords right — it's fine to miss on the first attempt at a new root and study the correct spelling the widget reveals. The goal isn't speed yet; it's trusting the pattern enough that you stop needing to think "what note is this" and start thinking "root, up 4, up 3, up 4."
