The ii–V–I Progression

Three chords you already know — min7, dom7, maj7 — strung together in one key. This one move powers most of jazz.
Why this, now? You have all three 7th-chord colors (Lesson 2, Lesson 3). Today you put them in motion: the ii–V–I, the single most common chord progression in jazz — it shows up dozens of times in almost every standard. Once this feels natural in a couple of keys, you're ready to start reading real lead sheets. (See your mission.)

The idea, in 90 seconds

Chords in a key get labeled by roman numerals counted from "home" — the tonic, numeral I. Lowercase means a minor chord, uppercase means major or dominant. The three you'll use constantly: ii (minor 7th, a whole step — 2 half steps — above the tonic), V (dominant 7th, a 5th — 7 half steps — above the tonic), and I (major 7th, the tonic itself).

iiDm7— sets up the motion
VG7— tension, wants to resolve
ICmaj7— home

"ii sets up, V creates tension, I is home. Wherever your tonic starts, ii always sits a whole step above it and V always sits a 5th above it — same two numbers, every key."

In the key of C, that's exactly the shape above: D is a whole step above C, so ii = Dm7. G is a 5th above C, so V = G7. And I is Cmaj7 itself — the chord shapes you already know, just placed at specific spots relative to home. (The root motion — up a 4th each time, D→G→C — is called the circle of fourths; it's the engine behind most jazz harmony, but that's a story for later.)

Now you try

Each round names a key and a role (ii, V, or I) — build that chord using the shape it calls for (min7 for ii, dom7 for V, maj7 for I). You'll do a full ii–V–I in C first, since you already know those three chords, then transfer the same +2/+7 formula to a new key, F, to prove it generalizes.

Check yourself

Getting the C round right just confirms chords you already knew. The real test is F: given only "F is the tonic," can you find ii and V from the +2/+7 formula without being told the letter names? That transfer is the actual skill — it's what lets you handle a lead sheet in any key, not just the ones you've memorized.
