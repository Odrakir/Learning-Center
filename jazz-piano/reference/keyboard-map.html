Keyboard Map

The pattern that lets you find any note without counting from the edge of the piano.

Two octaves, C3–B4

The pattern: black keys come in groups of 2, then 3, repeating all the way up and down the piano. Find any group of 2 black keys — the white key just to their left is always C. That's it; that's the whole trick. No counting from the end of the keyboard, ever.

Reading it

White keys are the seven natural letter names, A through G, repeating. Black keys sit between certain white keys and are named relative to their neighbors (sharps/flats) — see the glossary.

Notice two white-key pairs with no black key between them: E–F and B–C. Those are the two places the black-key pattern "breaks" — and it's exactly what creates the 2-then-3 grouping.

Middle C (marked above) is the C nearest the center of a full 88-key piano — the shared reference point musicians use when talking about pitch.
