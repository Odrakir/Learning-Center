Jazz Piano — Glossary

The shared vocabulary for this workspace. Terms here are used consistently across every lesson. Grows lesson by lesson.

Keyboard geography

Octave
The span from one note to the next note of the same letter name (e.g. C to the next C). On a full 88-key piano there are just over 7 of them. Same letter, same "color" of sound, different pitch height.
Middle C
The C nearest the middle of a full-size keyboard — the anchor pitch musicians use as a shared reference point. Found via the black-key pattern, not by counting from the edge of the keyboard.
Natural note
Any of the seven white-key letter names: A, B, C, D, E, F, G. The alphabet resets after G back to A.
Sharp (♯) / flat (♭)
A black key, named relative to the white key next to it. The same black key is both the sharp of the note below it and the flat of the note above it (e.g. C♯ = D♭) — which name is used depends on context, covered in a later lesson.
Half step (semitone)
The smallest distance between two notes on a piano — one key to the very next key, black or white, with nothing in between.
Whole step (tone)
Two half steps. Most, but not all, neighboring white keys are a whole step apart (E–F and B–C are the exceptions — only a half step, no black key between them).

Chords (introduced from Lesson 2 onward)

Interval
The distance between two notes, measured in steps. Chords are built by stacking specific intervals on top of a root note.
7th chord
A four-note chord — the basic building block of jazz harmony (as opposed to the three-note "triads" of most beginner piano method books). The three core colors: major 7th, minor 7th, and dominant 7th.
Voicing
A specific way of arranging a chord's notes on the keyboard (which notes, which octave, which hand). The same chord can be voiced many ways; jazz players choose voicings for sound and hand comfort, not just theoretical correctness.
