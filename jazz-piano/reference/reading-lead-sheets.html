Reading a Lead Sheet

A one-glance reference: the parts of a lead sheet, and the landmark notes on the treble staff. Print it and keep it by the keyboard.

The two things a lead sheet gives you

A lead sheet is a song stripped to essentials: the melody on the staff, and chord symbols above it. Everything else — the exact left-hand voicing, the rhythm you comp, the bass line — is yours to supply. That freedom is the point.

Melody on the staff · chord symbols above each bar · bar lines split the beats into measures · a thick final barline ends the section.

Landmark notes — middle C up to B

You don't need to read every note cold. Anchor on middle C (one ledger line below the staff) and step up the alphabet, alternating line, space, line, space.

Two worth memorising as anchors: E = bottom line, G = second line up, B = middle line. Count to any other note from the nearest anchor.

Full shapes and interval patterns in the 7th Chord Shapes reference. The bare number with no word (C7) always means dominant.

How to read a bar, in order

  1. Key & time — glance at the clef and time signature once, at the start.
  2. Chord symbol — read the letters above the bar; play that shape underneath.
  3. Melody — read each staff note from your nearest landmark; that's the tune on top.
  4. Look for the ii–V–I — spotting a Dm7 → G7 → Cmaj7 pattern tells you the whole passage at a glance.
