Electronics · Reference

Course Glossary

Every term this course uses, defined once and used consistently. Terms marked soon haven't been taught yet — they're listed so the road ahead is visible.
Where a definition here disagrees with something you read elsewhere, tell me. Hobby electronics writing is full of loose language, and pinning down which version we're using is worth more than either version alone.

The fundamentals

ChargeQ · coulomb (C)L1
The underlying stuff that moves. You will almost never compute with it directly, but current and voltage are both defined in terms of it.
VoltageV · volt (V)L1
The difference in electrical potential between two points — the “pressure” that pushes charge. Always measured between two points. Also called potential difference, or “volts across”.
CurrentI · ampere (A)L1
The rate at which charge flows past a point. In a single loop it is the same everywhere in that loop. Said as “current through”, never “current across”.
ResistanceR · ohm (Ω)L1
How strongly a component opposes current. A property of the part itself, independent of the circuit it sits in.
Ohm's lawL1
I = V / R, with rearrangements V = I × R and R = V / I. Exact for resistive components; an approximation or plainly false for others.
PowerP · watt (W)L1
Energy delivered per second: P = V × I. In a resistor it all leaves as heat, which is why parts have wattage ratings.
CircuitL1
A complete conducting loop from a source, through components, and back. Break the loop anywhere and current everywhere in it stops.
Short circuitL1
A path of near-zero resistance across a source. Ohm's law then predicts enormous current — the failure mode behind most melted parts and fires.
LoadL1
Whatever the source is driving — the thing that consumes the power. Your motors are the robot's main load.
Conventional currentL1
The agreed convention that current flows from + to −. Electrons in a metal actually drift the other way; the convention predates that discovery and every schematic and datasheet uses it. Follow the convention.

Components

ResistorL1
A component made to have a specific, stable resistance. Used to limit current, divide voltage, and set the operating point of other parts.
Voltage sourceL1
Something that holds a fixed voltage across its terminals and lets the circuit decide the current — a battery, to a good approximation.
Series / parallelsoon
The two ways to connect components: end-to-end sharing one current, or side-by-side sharing one voltage.
Voltage dividersoon
Two resistors in series used to produce a fraction of the input voltage. The circuit underneath most simple sensors.
Diode / LEDsoon
A component that conducts in one direction only, and does not obey Ohm's law. An LED is a diode that emits light while conducting.
Capacitorsoon
Stores charge and resists sudden voltage change. Used to steady a supply when motors cause it to sag.
Transistorsoon
A component in which a small signal controls a large current. The reason a microcontroller pin can command a motor it could never power directly.
H-bridgesoon
Four switches arranged so a motor's current can be sent either way through it — how a robot reverses a wheel.
Motor driversoon
A packaged H-bridge plus protection, taking logic-level inputs. The robot will likely use a TB6612FNG or an L298N module.

Control and signals

Microcontrollersoon
A small computer on one chip, with pins it can read and drive directly. The robot's brain.
GPIOsoon
General-purpose input/output — a pin the program can either read as a digital input or drive HIGH/LOW as an output.
PWMsoon
Pulse-width modulation: switching a pin on and off rapidly, varying the on-fraction. How you get a range of motor speeds from a pin that can only be fully on or fully off.
Pull-up / pull-downsoon
A resistor that holds an input at a known level when nothing else is driving it — the fix for an input that reads noise.
GroundGNDsoon
The shared reference point all voltages are measured against. Two circuits that must talk to each other need a common ground.

The robot

Differential drivesoon
Two independently driven wheels plus a caster. Steer by driving the wheels at different speeds; spin in place by driving them opposite ways.
Cliff / edge sensorsoon
A downward-facing sensor that detects the absence of a surface. Non-optional here: it is what stops the robot driving off the table.
Stall currentsoon
The current a motor draws when it is powered but prevented from turning — its worst case, and the number that sizes the driver and battery.
Power budgetsoon
The sum of what every part draws, against what the battery can supply and for how long. Decided before the build, not after.
