Ohm's Law & Units

The one equation, its three forms, the unit prefixes that cause every beginner arithmetic error, and the sanity-check numbers worth memorising.

The equation, three ways

I = V / R
current from V and R
V = I × R
voltage from I and R
R = V / I
resistance from V and I

Cover the quantity you want; what's left is the formula.

Quantities and units

QuantitySymbolUnitIs
VoltageVvolt (V) Pressure difference between two points. Never absolute.
CurrentIampere (A) Rate of charge flow past a point. Same everywhere in one loop.
ResistanceRohm (Ω) Opposition to flow. A property of the part itself.
PowerPwatt (W) Energy per second. In a resistor, all of it becomes heat.

Power follows directly: P = V × I, and by substitution P = I²R = V²/R. Reach for it whenever the question is “will this get hot?”

Prefixes — where the factor-of-1000 errors live

PrefixSymbolMultiplierTypical use
millim×0.001 mA — almost every current you'll meet
kilok×1 000 kΩ — most resistors in signal circuits
megaM×1 000 000 MΩ — pull-ups, meter input impedance
microµ×0.000 001 µF — capacitors (a later lesson)
The one rule Convert to plain volts, amps and ohms before using the equation, and convert back afterwards. 20 mA is 0.02 A. 4.7 kΩ is 4700 Ω. Nearly every wrong answer in this course will trace back to skipping this step.

Numbers worth knowing by heart

ThingRough figureWhy it matters
Indicator LED current10–20 mA The reference point for “small current”.
Microcontroller pin, max~20–40 mA Exceed it and you damage the chip. A motor is far past this — hence a driver.
Small hobby DC motor, running100–300 mA Already an order of magnitude beyond a pin.
Same motor, stalled1–2 A Stall resistance is tiny, so current spikes. Sizes your driver and your battery.
Standard resistor rating0.25 W Above this it cooks. Check with P = V × I.
Common logic supplies3.3 V / 5 V Mixing them without thought is a classic way to kill a part.
These are order-of-magnitude anchors, not datasheet values. When a real part is in front of you, the datasheet wins — always.

How to attack any DC question

  1. Draw the loop. Mark which two points a voltage is across.
  2. Convert everything to V, A, Ω.
  3. Ask what you know and what you want; pick the matching form.
  4. Compute, then sanity-check the magnitude against the table above. Milliamps or amps? If a signal circuit came out at 3 A, you slipped a thousand somewhere.
  5. Check the heat: P = V × I. Over a quarter watt, think again.
